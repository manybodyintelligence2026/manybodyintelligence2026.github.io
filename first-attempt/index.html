First attempt & First result

One Claude Code skill for the hours of building: /board-to-latex turns a photo of a whiteboard, blackboard or paper derivation into faithful, compilable LaTeX, and acts on the commands you write next to it on the board: Calculate this!, Check this! and Feynman!

Core idea — ask, never guess.
Clean typeset LaTeX looks authoritative, so a silently guessed index, sign or factor of 2 becomes almost invisible and can corrupt a whole derivation. The skill transcribes exactly what is written, marks anything it cannot read with a red ?n and asks you, and never corrects your physics inside the transcription: possible errors go into a separate note.
Getting started (3 steps):
  1. Download & let Claude install (30 seconds): click Download the skill (.zip) below, then paste the install prompt right under it into Claude Code — it goes into your personal ~/.claude/skills/ folder. Never edit your team repo's .claude/ folder.
  2. Photograph the board into photos/: derivations on paper or a whiteboard are part of the day's record. To have something calculated, checked or drawn, circle it and write the command next to it before you take the photo.
  3. Transcribe: in Claude Code, ask /board-to-latex to transcribe the photo and save the .tex in your own folder. Answer its questions about the red ?n markers; it then carries out the commands and gives you the updated LaTeX (and a PDF when it drew a diagram).

Board commands

Circle the expression, derivation or diagram and write the command next to it (any capitalisation). A circle or box without a command is only a highlight.

Write on the board What the skill does
Calculate this! Evaluates the circled expression numerically in Python (CODATA constants from SciPy), states the values and units it used, and sanity-checks dimensions, limits and order of magnitude. Missing values or units are asked for, not assumed.
Check this! Verifies the circled derivation step by step, symbolically with SymPy where it can and by numerical spot checks where it cannot, and gives each step a verdict: ✓ follows, ✗ does not follow (with where the discrepancy appears), or ? cannot verify.
Feynman! Draws the circled Feynman diagram with tikz-feynman exactly as you drew it (your topology, line types, arrows and labels only), compiles it and compares the picture with the photo.

A Check this! verdict on a derivation a person made is an independent check: it can go into checks/checks.md with method re-derivation and done by second-AI. Log the transcription with /log-entry like any other task.

What it will not do

  • Guess a symbol it cannot read, or which of two readings you meant (ν or v, a prime or a smudge): it asks.
  • Correct your physics inside the transcription. A suspected sign error is transcribed as written and mentioned in the notes.
  • Calculate or check anything that still contains an unread symbol.
  • Draw a diagram, plot or sketch you did not ask for: it leaves a placeholder and asks.

For the PDF you need LaTeX with the physics, slashed and tikz-feynman packages (all in TeX Live, MacTeX and MiKTeX). Without LaTeX, paste the .tex into Overleaf.

Starter prompts

One photo:

Use /board-to-latex on photos/[board-1.jpg]. Save the .tex in my folder.

A board in several photos:

Use /board-to-latex on photos/[board-left.jpg] and photos/[board-right.jpg]: they are the left and right halves of one board.

After its questions:

Answers: 1 is ν, 2 is a prime, 3 is a lower index. Update the .tex and carry out the commands that were waiting.

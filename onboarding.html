Code of conduct

Important: read it before the hackathon! Some points from the page:

No confidential data in AI toolsThat includes unpublished data from collaborators and anything covered by an agreement with an outside partner.
Check what the AI gives youA result only counts once someone on the team has checked it. Say how in your write-up.
Keep a log in your team's repositoryWrite down what you asked, which model, what came back, what you checked and what didn't work. Team repositories are private.
Ask before using other people's ideas, code or computersIdeas from your group's plans or a grant need your PI's OK. So does code or computing time that belongs to your group or someone else.
Co-authorship of the event paper is optionalYou can change your mind until the paper is submitted.
Results belong to the teamIf you write a paper from it later, everyone who worked on it is offered co-authorship.

Finding a research idea

  1. Everyone writes a few lines about themselves: group, background, the methods they know.
  2. Everyone adds 2–3 of their papers as PDFs. Download the PDFs. Claude works much better with files than with links.
  3. Open the folder in Claude and use the prompt on the next slide.
our-idea/ +-- team.md a paragraph each +-- papers/ +-- name-1/ 2-3 PDFs +-- name-2/ +-- name-3/

Prompt: finding a project

We are a team of [number] researchers in the Materials Theory division at Uppsala University, taking part in Many-Body Intelligence 2026: a one-day hackathon (Monday 30 November, 09:00-17:00) where small teams pick a research problem none of them has worked on and see how far they get with AI assistants. Each team ends the day with a 5-minute talk and a 2-page note.

This folder has team.md (a short description of each of us) and papers/ (a few papers by each of us). Read all of it first, and the event's pages at manybodyintelligence2026.github.io (home, FAQ and code of conduct).

Then propose 5 projects that draw on the interests and skills of everyone on the team. Each one should be:
- the size of a Master's project (it could become a short paper with more time)
- new to all of us (existing codes and methods are fine)
- free to share, not part of a group's plans or a grant
- doable on our laptops
- able to give a first result, however rough, by 17:00 on the day

For each: a title, the question in 2-3 sentences, what a first result by 17:00 could look like, likely methods, and what each of us would own on the day. Check that none overlaps with our own or our groups' current research, and look for the closest existing work.

Then recommend one, with the checks we should run and the main risks, and list the ideas you dropped and why. Put the result in an artifact we can share.

Agree on one idea

  1. Pick one idea and write it up in about 100 words: title, question, what a first result by 17:00 could look like, methods.
  2. Share it in your team's Discord channel. The easiest way is as a Claude artifact. An artifact is a page Claude makes (a document, a table, a small app) that you can share as a link. Ask Claude to make one.
  3. Everyone on the team reads it and agrees to it before 30 November.

We're happy to give feedback if you want it. Stuck? Ask us for a starter problem.

Three connections

DiscordSo we can reach you, and you can reach us
ClaudeSo you can work with your AI agent
GitHubSo the AI agents can talk to each other

1 · Discord

  1. Join the server with the invite link in your email.
  2. Post a short intro in #welcome: who you are, your research group and your hackathon team.
  3. In #team-selection, choose your team. You'll then see your team's channel.
DONE WHEN your intro is in #welcome and you can see your team's channel.

2 · Claude

  1. Log in to Claude with the account on the division's plan. Tell us now if you can't.
  2. Install Claude Code: the Claude desktop app (Windows, Mac), or VS Code with the Claude Code extension (Linux too). On Windows, also install Git for Windows.
  3. Make a folder for the hackathon on the laptop you'll bring, and open it in Claude Code.
  4. Ask Claude a question there to check that it works.
DONE WHEN Claude replies to you in your hackathon folder.

3 · GitHub

  1. Accept the invite to your team's repository (private, in manybodyintelligence2026).
  2. In Claude Code, in your hackathon folder, paste the prompt on the next slide. Claude installs what's missing, logs you in to GitHub (you do the browser step), copies the repository and adds a folder with your name.
  3. From then on, your team's Claudes share work through the repository: the files, a CLAUDE.md describing the project, and the team log.
DONE WHEN your folder shows up in your team's repository on GitHub.

Prompt: connecting to GitHub

I'm taking part in a hackathon, and my team works in this GitHub repository: [paste the link to your team's repository]
Please set things up so that you can work with it:

1. Check that git and the GitHub CLI (gh) are installed. If they aren't, install them for my operating system (on Windows: Git for Windows and the GitHub CLI), and tell me if I need to open a new terminal afterwards.
2. Log me in to GitHub with "gh auth login": GitHub.com, HTTPS, log in with a web browser. I'll do the browser step myself.
3. Clone the repository into this folder.
4. Make a folder in it named [your name], with a README.md saying who I am. Commit it and push it (pull first if the push is refused).
5. When it's done, give me the link to my folder on GitHub.

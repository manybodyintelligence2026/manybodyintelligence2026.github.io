The day

Read the code of conduct

Everyone confirms they have read it. The parts that matter before you start:

Keep confidential things out of AI toolsNo unpublished data from collaborators, nothing under an agreement with an outside partner.
Check what the AI gives youA result counts once someone on the team has checked it, and the write-up says how.
Keep a log in your team's repositoryWhat you asked, which model, what came back, what you checked, what didn't work. Team repositories are private.
Bring only what you are free to shareIdeas in your group's plans or a grant need your PI's agreement; code or computers that belong to your group or someone else need permission.
Co-authorship of the event paper is opt-inYou can change your mind until it is submitted.
Results belong to the teamEveryone who worked on it is offered co-authorship of a project paper.

Your research idea: ask Claude

  1. Each of you writes a short description: who you are, your group, your background, the methods you know.
  2. Each of you adds 2–3 of your papers as PDFs. Download them into the folder rather than giving Claude links: it keeps the content of files much better than of links.
  3. Give Claude the folder and the prompt on the next slide: the event, the rules for a good project, and a request for ideas that draw on everyone.
our-idea/ +-- team.md a paragraph each +-- papers/ +-- name-1/ 2-3 PDFs +-- name-2/ +-- name-3/

The prompt

We are a team of [number] researchers in the Materials Theory division at Uppsala University, taking part in Many-Body Intelligence 2026: a one-day hackathon (Monday 30 November, 09:00-17:00) where small teams pick a research problem none of them has worked on and see how far they get with AI assistants. Each team ends the day with a 5-minute talk and a 2-page note.

This folder has team.md (a short description of each of us) and papers/ (a few papers by each of us). Read all of it first, and the event's pages at manybodyintelligence2026.github.io (home, FAQ and code of conduct).

Then propose 5 projects that draw on the interests and skills of everyone on the team. Each one should be:
- the size of a Master's project (it could become a short paper with more time)
- new to all of us (existing codes and methods are fine)
- free to share, not part of a group's plans or a grant
- doable on our laptops
- able to give a first result, however rough, by 17:00 on the day

For each: a title, the question in 2-3 sentences, what a first result by 17:00 could look like, likely methods, and what each of us would own on the day. Check that none overlaps with our own or our groups' current research, and look for the closest existing work.

Then recommend one, with the checks we should run and the main risks, and list the ideas you dropped and why. Put the result in an artifact we can share.

Agree on it before the 30th

  1. Pick one idea and write it up in about 100 words: title, question, what a first result by 17:00 could look like, methods.
  2. Share it in your team's Discord channel. A Claude artifact is an easy way: an artifact is a page Claude makes for you (a document, a table, even a small app) that you can share with a link. Ask Claude to put the write-up in an artifact, then share it.
  3. Everyone on the team reads it and agrees before Monday 30 November.

Want our feedback on it? Ask us; it's optional. No idea yet? We have a few starter problems.

3 · GitHub

  1. Accept the invite to your team's repository (private, in manybodyintelligence2026).
  2. In Claude Code, in your hackathon folder, paste the prompt on the next slide. Claude installs what's missing, logs you in to GitHub (you do the browser step), copies the repository and adds a folder with your name.
  3. The Claudes on your team then share work through the repository: its files, a CLAUDE.md describing the project, and the team's log.
CHECK Your folder shows up in your team's repository on GitHub.

The setup prompt

I'm taking part in a hackathon, and my team works in this GitHub repository: [paste the link to your team's repository]
Please set things up so that you can work with it:

1. Check that git and the GitHub CLI (gh) are installed. If they aren't, install them for my operating system (on Windows: Git for Windows and the GitHub CLI), and tell me if I need to open a new terminal afterwards.
2. Log me in to GitHub with "gh auth login": GitHub.com, HTTPS, log in with a web browser. I'll do the browser step myself.
3. Clone the repository into this folder.
4. Make a folder in it named [your name], with a README.md saying who I am. Commit it and push it (pull first if the push is refused).
5. When it's done, give me the link to my folder on GitHub.

Scope

One Claude Code skill for the first hour of the day: /scope-to-one-day turns your team's idea into plan.md, a plan that fits the day, so that the hour goes on talking physics with each other rather than on writing the plan.

Core idea — an honest plan beats an ambitious one.
The most common way to lose a hackathon day is to start three heavy calculations and finish none. The skill makes the options and their costs clear, pushes back when something will not fit in about three hours of building, and writes the result down. Your team makes the scientific decisions.
Getting started at 09:15 (3 steps):
  1. Download & let Claude install (30 seconds): click Download the skill (.zip) below, then paste the install prompt right under it into Claude Code — Claude unzips it into your personal ~/.claude/skills/ folder. Never edit your team repo's .claude/ folder (that holds the hackathon's /log-entry and /log-check tools).
  2. Run it in your team repository: git pull so you see everything your teammates put in our-idea/, start a new Claude Code session there and type /scope-to-one-day. One person runs it, with the team around the screen.
  3. Decide together: answer its questions, choose the question and who takes which track, confirm the lines it proposes for README.md and give your own Confidence. It commits plan.md; everyone else runs git pull.

What it does

Step What happens
1 · Find the idea Reads the Q0 line in README.md, our-idea/ and the names of your papers. Several candidates and no decision: compares the strongest two or three, recommends one and asks you to choose. No idea yet: says so and offers two or three.
2 · Restate the question One sentence naming the system or model, what is varied and what is computed. Checks it against the event's rules: about a Master's project, new to everyone on the team, free to share, laptop-sized, a first result possible by the end of the day.
3 · Feasibility audit Rough numbers for the core calculation (matrix size, k-points, parameter points, time per point) against about three hours of building. A chain of heavy steps is cut to one core step, with the others taken from published values or a simpler model. Verdict: fits / fits if narrowed / does not fit.
4 · Literature check Suggests one person runs it in parallel: has the question been answered, the closest results, one known result to reproduce first as a benchmark. Cites only what it can identify by DOI or arXiv number.
5 · “Done” at three levels Minimum (end of First result), Target (before Check), Stretch (only after Check): each a figure or an equation you could put on a slide.
6 · Fallbacks Two or three narrower questions, each with a trigger tied to a stage of the day, so that nobody has to re-scope under pressure.
7 · Checks, chosen now The checks for the Check phase, chosen before any result exists, each with its expected outcome; at least one done by a person.
8 · Split the work One track per person with an output and a hand-over point. Tracks that share equations agree on conventions first, in the Conventions section of README.md.
9 · Write plan.md One page, committed. It shows you the lines for the question history in README.md (Q1, “Done today means”, “Expected”) and writes them only once you agree.

What it will not do

  • Choose your question or decide who does what: it recommends, you decide.
  • Fill in “Confidence (1–5)” in README.md: that is your team's judgement.
  • Invent a project when our-idea/ is empty, or cite a paper it cannot identify.

What plan.md looks like

# <title>: plan for the day

Question: <one sentence>
Verdict: <fits / fits if narrowed / does not fit>. Cost: <the numbers>.

## Done           Minimum · Target · Stretch
## Fallbacks      If <trigger tied to a stage> → <narrower question>
## Benchmark      the known result to reproduce first
## Literature     references with DOI or arXiv number
## Checks         check · expected outcome · method · done by
## Tracks         track · who · output · hand-over
## Conventions    units, Hamiltonian, lattice, Fourier convention
## Assumptions and open questions

Starter prompts

Typing /scope-to-one-day is enough. Telling it who is here and what you can run saves a round of questions:

Use /scope-to-one-day. Today we are [names]. [Name] knows [code or method], [name] knows [...].
We have laptops only.

If you have several ideas and have not chosen:

Use /scope-to-one-day. We have several candidate ideas in our-idea/ and have not chosen yet.
Compare the strongest two or three and recommend one.

Later in the day, when things do not go to plan:

We are at the end of First result and nothing works yet. Re-read plan.md,
apply the matching fallback and update the plan with what changed and why.

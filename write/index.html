Write

Two Claude Code skills for the last stage before the talks: /check-results checks what you are about to claim, and /write-note turns your repository into the two-page note: a PRL-style letter, built as a PDF, under the note's eight headings.

Core idea — check, then write.
Every number in the note should be checked before it is written: a note built on an unchecked result spreads the error into a document that looks authoritative. And the note is an argument, not a log. It becomes one section of the joint paper, so it has to say what you found, how you checked it, and how AI was used, in two pages.
Getting started at 15:00 (3 steps):
  1. Download & let Claude install (30 seconds): click Download both skills (.zip) below, then paste the install prompt right under it into Claude Code — both skills go into your personal ~/.claude/skills/ folder. Never edit your team repo's .claude/ folder.
  2. Check: one person runs /check-results, best in a fresh session so that its checks count as independent (second-AI). It re-runs your scripts on copies, compares every number with the code and the papers, and writes note/results.md. Add the check rows you agree with to checks/checks.md.
  3. Write: run /write-note. It writes note/note.tex, builds the two-page note/note.pdf and traces every number in note/provenance.md; you edit. Fill in the question-history lines, section 8 and CONTRIBUTIONS.md yourselves, then everyone runs /log-check.

What each skill does

Skill What it does
/check-results Checks your results the way a referee who also re-runs the code would: re-runs the scripts on copies (never in your folders), compares every number you quote with the code, opens the cited papers, re-derives the key steps and looks for convention clashes between people's tracks. Findings come classified (error, inconsistency, attribution, decision, ...) with their evidence. Writes note/results.md: each result with its value, its source and how it was checked, plus proposed rows for the checks table.
/write-note Writes the note as a two-page PRL-style letter (revtex4-2, two columns: title, abstract, one figure, the checks table as Table I, references), with the eight headings as its run-in heads, from what the repository holds: the question history from README.md, the checked results, the checks table, your AI logs and CONTRIBUTIONS.md. It builds note/note.pdf, checks that sections 2, 5 and 8 match their sources and that the letter fits two pages, and traces every number in note/provenance.md.

What they will not do

  • Write your question history, add rows to your checks table, or answer section 8: those are yours. They propose; you decide.
  • Describe the AI's work more favourably than your log entries do, or fill in a judgement the logs do not contain.
  • Edit or move a teammate's scripts and figures without asking.
  • Present an unchecked result as checked, or cite a paper they cannot identify by DOI or arXiv number.

Where each section of the note comes from

Section Source
Title and abstractThe main result as a claim; the abstract in at most 600 characters
1 · Title, team ID, membersYour names; the team ID from event.json
2 · Question historyCopied unchanged from README.md
3 · Approach and methodsYour code, parameters with their sources, conventions
4 · Main resultnote/results.md; one figure; what was checked and what not
5 · Checks tableTable I, copied unchanged from checks/checks.md
6 · How AI was usedYour log entries in log/P##.md, and only those
7 · What would come nextThe stretch goal, the fallback not taken, the open question
8 · Requested treatmentCopied from CONTRIBUTIONS.md

A complete example note (PDF) shows the result: a made-up team, with real numbers from a one-second calculation on the Haldane model. Its LaTeX source and provenance file are in the skill.

You need LaTeX with revtex4-2 (TeX Live, MacTeX or MiKTeX) to build the PDF on your laptop. Without it, /write-note still writes note/note.tex: upload it with the figure PDF to a new Overleaf project, compile there and save the PDF as note/note.pdf.

Starter prompts

Check the result the note will rest on first:

Use /check-results. Our main result is [the figure or number]; check that first, then the rest.

Then write:

Use /write-note. Our main result is [one sentence].

When it is too long:

Shorten the note to two pages. Keep sections 2, 5 and 8 exactly as they are, and keep what was and was not checked.

No LaTeX on your laptop:

Use /write-note. We have no LaTeX installed: write note/note.tex and tell us how to build the PDF on Overleaf.
